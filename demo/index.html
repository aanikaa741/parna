What you are looking at

The standard detector finds onsets in the mixed recording, then sorts them into two groups by sound. When the client plays in time, both hits land within a few milliseconds and it hears one hit, so the therapist's beat drops out exactly when the client is doing well, and the timing it reports is off by hundreds of milliseconds.

The calibrated method first learns each instrument from the five solo taps, then splits the recording into "how much drum" and "how much shaker" at every moment (non-negative matrix factorisation). Hits that land together stay separate.

This is simulated audio: a modelled room, synthesised instruments and a text-to-speech therapist, with every hit time known exactly. Across 60 such sessions the calibrated method measured client timing within a median 3 to 4 ms for different-sounding instruments; two identical drums remain unsolved.
