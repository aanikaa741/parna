parna

For the ones who speak in rhythm.

Who kept the beat? Parna measures how a therapist and client play together, from a single phone recording: whether they joined in, how closely they kept time, and who was leading. It's built for music therapists, who today judge this by ear and write it up from memory.

01 ✦ the problem

Keeping the beat is therapy

Matching a shared rhythm is a common goal for autistic children and in neurologic rehab. Therapists have to document progress, but there's no easy way to measure it from an ordinary session with real drums.

session_note.txt

Written from memory

A 2023 study found that collecting and documenting outcomes is a real burden in medical music therapy. "Client kept the beat better today" is a judgement call.

Two players, one beat

The therapist plays a steady beat on a frame drum. The client follows on a shaker. When the client is in time, their hits land within a few milliseconds of the therapist's.

Calibration is the trick

Five solo taps per player at the start (two turned out to be enough) let the tool separate the instruments, even when they hit at the same moment.

05 ✦ who's leading?

Lead or follow

In free play the therapist often follows the client on purpose, to meet them where they are. So the tool also asks who is adapting to whom. Each player's next hit leans toward the other's last one, and whoever leans less is leading. Pick a leader and watch the tool work it out from the hits alone.
